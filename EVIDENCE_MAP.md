## Why Schoenfeld 2019 and de Camargo 2026 are NOT in the quantitative synthesis despite having real, verified numbers

This is a deliberate methodological decision, investigated (not assumed) this round:

- **Schoenfeld 2019**: this project mathematically reverse-engineered the likely formula behind the authors' reported effect size (see `analysis/verify_schoenfeld_es.py`) and found it is standardized against the *average pre-training SD pooled across all three treatment groups* - a fundamentally different metric from the *own-group pre-to-post Cohen's d* that Radaelli, Heaselgrave, and Aube each report and that the current figure plots. Even now that the formula is understood, these two types of ES are not directly interchangeable without recomputing one to match the other's convention - which has not been done, to avoid introducing a project-computed statistic not verified against the source.
- **de Camargo 2026**: reports a *between-protocol* effect size on a *relative-to-habitual-volume* design (comparing +20% to +120% change from each participant's own baseline), not an *absolute weekly-set, within-group* effect size like the figure's 3 studies. The outcome scale and the underlying research question (relative increase vs. absolute volume level) are different enough that merging them would misrepresent both.

Both studies are discussed narratively, with their real numbers, in the README and manuscript - they are evidence, just not evidence poolable with the current figure without further methodological work this project has not yet done.
