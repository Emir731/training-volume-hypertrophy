# Research Notes — Correction and Discovery History

This file exists so the project's own development is traceable. Nothing here is invented after the fact — each entry reflects a real correction or discovery made during the work, in roughly the order it happened.

## Radaelli et al. (2015) — weekly-set count correction
An early draft of the extraction table reported %change values of 1.9/7.6/17.5% for this study, sourced from a secondary review, alongside a weekly-set count of 6/18/30. On attempting to verify this against the primary text directly, neither of those numbers could be confirmed — the primary source reports effect sizes (0.10/0.73/1.10 for elbow flexor MT across 1/3/5 sets-per-exercise), not percentage changes, and an initial reading of the primary methods section suggested a 3/9/15 weekly-set structure (1/3/5 sets of one exercise × 3 sessions/week). This was later found to be an incomplete reading: the design actually used 2 exercises for biceps and 3 for triceps, giving 6/18/30 (biceps) and 9/27/45 (triceps) weekly sets — a reading confirmed by convergent agreement across independent secondary sources describing the same design. The wrong %change values were removed rather than corrected in place, and the weekly-set correction is documented in the extraction table's notes field for that row rather than silently overwritten.

## Schoenfeld et al. (2019) — effect-size formula investigation
Once full text was obtained (including raw Table 1 means and SDs), this project computed its own within-group Cohen's d from the raw data and found it did not match the authors' own reported triceps effect sizes (0.12/0.30/0.55). Testing alternative denominators against the raw numbers found that dividing each group's mean change by the *simple average of all three groups' pre-training SDs* reproduces the authors' reported values to within 0.01 — a much closer fit than the change-score-SD convention used for this project's other comparable studies (which is off by 0.15-0.58 for the same data). This is documented as a strong inference, not a confirmed fact, in `analysis/verify_schoenfeld_es.py`, and is the specific, investigated reason Schoenfeld 2019 is excluded from the comparable quantitative figure — not a vague "different methods" hand-wave.

## Barbalho et al. (2019) — data-integrity discovery
While citation-chasing for related volume-dose-response literature, this project encountered a published critical commentary (Vigotsky, Nuckols, Heathers, Krieger, Schoenfeld & Steele, 2020, SportRxiv) documenting statistically improbable data patterns across a body of work by this research group. Checking further, the companion men's study was found to have been retracted at the authors' own request in 2020 (per Retraction Watch's reporting — this project has not independently retrieved the journal's own retraction notice). The women's study is not confirmed retracted but is covered by the same critique. Both studies are kept as rows in the extraction table (not deleted) with explicit E/F evidence-category status, so the evidence history remains visible even though neither contributes to synthesis.

## Systematic search — a tool-access limitation, not a shortcut
Attempting to run a genuinely systematic search revealed that this project's available tools cannot reproduce a true database session — there is no way to obtain an exact "N records retrieved" the way logging into PubMed or Scopus directly would give. Rather than presenting an approximate number as if it were a real database count, this project documents exactly what was searched, how, and what limitation this creates (`SYSTEMATIC_SEARCH.md`). Two existing, real, rigorously-executed systematic reviews of closely related scope (Baz-Valle et al. 2022; Pelland et al. 2025/26) are used as contextual benchmarks for the likely size of this literature, not merged into this project's own dataset.

## Evidence classification — from an ad hoc A-G scheme to a canonical A-I taxonomy
An earlier round of this project used an evidence-category scheme (A-G) that conflated "verified with usable quantitative data" with "comparable to the current synthesis figure." These are now tracked as two separate fields: `evidence_category` (A-I, a verification/integrity classification) and `comparability_to_figure` (a separate, rule-based comparability judgment computed in `analysis/comparability.py`). This split exists because a study can be fully primary-source-verified with real numbers (category A) and still not belong in the same figure as another category-A study, if their effect-size construction or outcome construct differs — which is exactly the situation with Schoenfeld 2019 and de Camargo 2026.
